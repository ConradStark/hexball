    How a turn works
    • Attack. Plan the whole turn at once: one pass or shot (two after a ground pass), and up to 3 players moving. A dribble counts as one of the 3. So: dribble then pass or shoot, or pass and the receiver dribbles on. Ground pass vs lob: a ground pass is easier to cut out, but the receiver plays it first time and can pass or shoot once more; a lob goes over defenders but he has to control it, and that's the turn's kick. One player can move twice (the second move up to 2 hexes) and is tired next time (−1 hex).
    • The pass or shot is one of: a pass to a teammate, a through ball into space, Give & Go (the passer runs up to 4 hexes for the return), the Cross card, or a shot. A pass to a runner is held until it will arrive with him; a through ball goes now and waits in the space. Give & Go and Cross rest for your next turn. Cross: from the outer two columns to a teammate in the box, who finishes first time (lob = header, low = shot).
    • Playback. Players move 1 hex a frame, ground passes 3, lobs land after 3 frames, shots go straight away. When planning you go around defenders; once play runs, a defender who steps into a run can't stop it (a dribbler gets a 1v1).
    • Defence. Prepare: once the attack is revealed (players, not the plan), move 2 players 1 hex each (the keeper counts). Step onto a loose ball and it's yours. React: every 2 frames you get a pause: mark one attacker (he's followed goal side for free) and send up to 3 defenders up to 2 hexes each, with 5 steps at the first pause and 2 plus whatever you saved after that (max 3). Undo takes back a move or mark before Go.
    • Winning the ball. A defender only contests the ball on a hex when it gets there: in a pass lane 40%, on the receiver 55%, on a dribbler about 45%. A defender who wins it clears it 60% of the time in his own box (25% elsewhere): it flies 3–4 hexes upfield and play stops. Whoever wins the ball has to bring players beyond the other side's last outfield player back level with him (onside).
    • The keeper stays in his box and uses his hands: he claims a pass to or through his hex (75%, 65% in the air), smothers a dribbler on his hex (55%) and rushes at one who comes next to him (30%). Get past him and he's out of the play. After a save or goal kick, attackers step out of his box.
    • Shooting. Closer and more central = easier to hit the target; tight angles and the far post from wide are hard. Defenders next to you put you off and can block; one in the line of your shot blocks that side. The keeper saves most in line with your aim and less a column or two away, and closes the angle when he's right on top of you. A keeper behind you can't save. Tap ⓘ on the goal for the numbers.
    • Ring colour shows rarity. Tap any player for his card. First to 3 goals wins.
